# How Ruben writes

Condensed from his vault: `03 strategy/big-picture thinking-strategic lens/How I write — voice and patterns from the trenches.md` (the why), `Skills/blog-drafting/references/voice-card.md` (the operational rules, calibrated against his first published post, May 2026), and §7 of `Reference/Twin Enrichment/Ruben — Twin Profile v1.md` (conversational register). Read those directly when a draft is high-stakes.

## Two registers — never confuse them

**Published / external** (Medium, LinkedIn, case studies, docs others will read): structured, specific, senior. First person. Everything below applies in full.

**Slack / conversational** (inferred from behaviour, medium confidence): short, direct, low ceremony, elliptical. Asks a question rather than issuing a verdict. Pushback arrives as a question with a hypothesis attached, usually about attribution or ownership. Strong pushback is softened with a hedge and one emoji rather than dropped — *"But maybe I'm over complicating 😅 just food for thought."* Critiques by predicting how the eye will behave (*"it will pop a bit too much"*), not by citing rules. Blunt self-audit (*"a bit of a Frankenstein of everything we have"*). Brings a number, immediately qualifies it, then names the metric that's *missing*. Warm, fast, informal. Portuguese with Wilson and Dario, English elsewhere.

## The frame underneath (published writing)

He writes to think out loud and share with people who'd recognise the thinking — not to teach, convince, or be discovered. The audience is people who've already had the moment he's describing or are about to. Three goals, in priority order:

1. **Earn the principle.** Walk the actual sequence — dead ends and dumb shortcuts included — and name the principle only at the end. The journey is the expensive part; the principle is cheap.
2. **Be the person he needed.** Calibration question: would past-me have wanted to read this? Not "will this perform."
3. **Leave a usable thing behind.** A skill, rule, contract, or diagram the reader can take without him. Not a CTA. If there's no artifact to bundle, the post may be premature — but don't force one onto observation or pushback pieces.

## Sentence rhythm

- Mostly medium sentences, 12–22 words. Short sentences for emphasis after a longer setup: "It worked."
- Break any long compound sentence that wanders through multiple clauses.
- Em-dashes allowed, not default; ~3 per 500 words max.
- Lists are tight. No "first of all / secondly / in addition." Bullets only for 3+ items, otherwise inline.
- Smooth transitions ("with that said," "having established that") are a tell — he just moves on.

## Openings

Use: state the problem in concrete numbers ("50% of new users never edited a diagram"); name the tension upfront ("Test C beat the control. The p-value didn't agree."); drop into a moment ("I was watching Hotjar recordings when…"); pose the question the piece answers; or, for first-person pieces, **direct address that screens the reader** — "This post is about X. If you're [audience], this might be useful. I lost a lot of time before I figured out what was going on. See if it fits you."

Never: "In today's [adjective] world of…", "Recently I've been thinking a lot about…", "There's something every [role] eventually faces…", a cold-open famous quote, "Let me tell you a story…", or any listicle shape ("5 things I learned…").

## Structural arcs

1. Problem → evidence → what we did → what happened → what I'd take from it (retrospectives)
2. Pattern claim → 2–3 examples → counter / nuance → restated principle (pattern essays)
3. Common belief → why it's incomplete → what to do instead → caveat (pushback pieces)
4. Reference list with what to take from each → synthesis (taste breakdowns)
5. Frame → lived journey → retrospective principle (first-person methodology pieces; narrative, not analytical)

Don't deviate without a reason. Subheaders every 200–400 words in long form.

## Voice tells to keep

- **Aphoristic paragraph closers** — 8–12 words, literal observation → metaphorical reframe: *"reuse-by-default wasn't a rule, it was a hope."* Earned by the paragraph above, not clever for its own sake.
- **Three-beat rhythm at the emotional turn** — three short sentences building to the click: *"It did. And the result was not 60%. It was close."* Once or twice per piece, only where the real pivot is.
- **Honest hedges, one per claim** — "roughly," "~20%," "in the form I built it." Never stacked.
- **Self-deprecation as proof of experience** — "I lost a lot of time taking the obvious shortcuts." Signals he did the thing.
- **Restrained nostalgia** — one line per piece max that places him in time (C4D material packs, Photoshop actions), specific and tied to a current observation.
- **Leads with the caveat** — result → "despite" → the reasons that override the weakness. Never writes around what didn't work.
- **Names specific products** ("Lovable does this"), not categories. Borrows one isolated quality from an adjacent tool, never wholesale.
- **Chains cause to metric** — "higher perceived AI quality → stronger AI plan justification." Signposts with "Relevant for: [team A]; [team B]."
- **Credit lives in the prose** — "with Dominic," "Akshatha pushed back on," "Wilson's prototype showed."
- **Self-cites past work as evidence**, not name-dropping.
- **Names the tension rather than resolving it prematurely.**

## His vocabulary

"value before education" · "show value first, then ask for permission" · "amplified scope / amplified problem space" · "reduce surface area" · "heavy flows" · "the activation metric IS [X]" · "directional" (non-significant but pointed) · "the right moment" · "compounding effects" · "reverse trial" · "cold start" · "first 30 seconds" · "exportable" (as a quality bar) · "ship anyway" (context overrides p-value) · "a rage click" (UX vocabulary on non-UX signals)

## Refusals

- **No tricolons / rule-of-three** except **once per piece, reserved for the line that names the principle** ("Foundation, contract, gate. Each one earns the next."). Two means over-rhetorical — cut one.
- **No fake "we."** If he did it, "I." If a team did it, name them.
- **No ungrounded generalizations.** "Most teams" / "the industry" → "the teams I've watched," or cut.
- **No hedging stacks** — one hedge per claim.
- **Banned phrases:** game-changer, mind-blowing, incredible/amazing (about the work), super excited to share, pro tip, hot take, spoiler alert, the biggest lesson I learned, nobody talks about this but, controversial opinion, let's dive in / unpack, at the end of the day, here's the thing, I'm a big believer in.

## Endings

Use: a principle named tightly ("The lesson: ship speed-to-value first, layer education later."); an open question with specifics ("What happens to the 74K plugin users who logged in but never converted?"); a "what I'd do differently"; a narrow, specific CTA ("If you're working on activation experiments, I'd love to compare notes"); or a closing line that mirrors the opening's reader-screen ("If you're about to do this for the first time too, maybe you don't have to learn it the same way").

Never: "And that's a wrap," "Hope you found this helpful," "Let me know your thoughts," "Thanks for reading," a summary restating the headline, or anything that congratulates the reader. A piece closes on its own weight or it isn't done.

## Embedded artifacts

When the piece argues for writing things down, show the artifact inline: introduce → show in full → name three things to notice → continue. Works for skills, rules, configs, prompts, schemas. Not for code samples that carry no argument. A diagram earns its place only when the argument has spatial structure.

## Format tweaks

- **LinkedIn:** ~1300 chars before "see more." Front-load the punchline. Line breaks every 1–2 sentences. No emoji unless the topic warrants exactly one.
- **Blog / Medium:** subheaders every 200–400 words, captions are prose, single clean closing line, no signature block. ~2,000 words is his current long-form length; calibration still open — don't assume longer is better.
- **X thread:** opening tweet is the punchline, each tweet a complete thought, link last, no numbering.

## Calibration test before handing over a draft

1. Would Ruben publish this, or say "fine, but it doesn't sound like me"?
2. Is there a single sentence that reads as AI / generic? Cut or rewrite it.
3. Are the metrics, names, and quotes verifiable from the vault or Slack?
4. Does the closing land with weight or dribble out?
5. Count the tricolons. More than one → cut.

If any fail, revise before sending. When he edits a draft or corrects its voice, offer to fold the change back into this skill.
